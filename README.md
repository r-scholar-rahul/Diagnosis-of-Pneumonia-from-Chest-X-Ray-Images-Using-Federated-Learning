2. What Is an “Awesome” Repository?

An Awesome-style repository is a carefully curated and well-organized collection of useful resources around  one topic. The main page is usually a structured README.md containing categorized links, short descriptions,  and navigation.

Example

Instead of creating a repository called “assignment-2”, a student working on Explainable AI may create  “awesome-explainable-ai” and organize resources under Survey Papers, Foundational Methods, Medical  Applications, Datasets, Tools, Implementations, and Tutorials.

For this assignment, you are creating an Awesome-style academic repository. You are not required to submit  it to the official Awesome project.

6. Research Paper Collection

Your repository must contain at least 20 verified scholarly papers. More papers do not automatically mean  better work. Quality, relevance, verification, and organization are more important than quantity.

Suggested Categories

- Survey and Review Papers

- Foundational Papers

- Recent Research Papers

- Methods / Algorithms

- Applications

- Evaluation Methods / Benchmarks

Required Format for Each Paper

- **Paper Title**

Authors, Year, Journal/Conference

[Paper / DOI](link)

One-line explanation of why the paper is relevant.

Do Not Paste Only URLs

Every listed paper must have enough information for another researcher to understand what it is and why it  belongs in your collection.

7. Citation and Resource Verification

Before adding a scholarly paper, verify the following:

- Correct title

- Correct authors

- Publication year

- Journal or conference

- DOI, where available

- Whether the paper genuinely exists

- Whether the provided link corresponds to the same paper

Recommended verification sources include publisher websites, DOI/Crossref, Google Scholar, Semantic  Scholar, PubMed (where relevant), and other authoritative scholarly databases.

Core Rule

Never accept a reference merely because an AI tool generated it. AI may help discover a resource, but you  are responsible for verifying it.

8. Minimum Supporting Resources

If datasets are genuinely not applicable to your topic, state this clearly in the repository and explain why.

9. How to Select Existing GitHub Repositories

Do not choose repositories only because they appear first in a search or have many stars. Examine:

- Documentation quality

- Availability and clarity of source code

- Recent maintenance/activity

- Examples or tutorials

- Reproducibility

- License

- Connection to a research paper or recognized project

10. Copyright and Ethical Use

Do not upload copyrighted PDFs of papers written by other authors unless redistribution is clearly permitted.  Prefer linking to the DOI, publisher page, PubMed record, arXiv page, or official open-access source.

You may upload your own assignment paper and your own citation-audit document.

14. Use of Generative AI

18. Important Rules

Rule 1: Do not fabricate references or resources.

Rule 2: Do not copy another student’s repository.

Rule 3: Do not blindly paste AI-generated lists.

Rule 4: Verify scholarly resources independently.

Rule 5: Do not upload copyrighted research-paper PDFs without permission.

Rule 6: Keep the repository public and accessible for evaluation.

Rule 7: Commit history should reflect genuine development.

Rule 8: The repository should be understandable to someone who has not attended the course.
